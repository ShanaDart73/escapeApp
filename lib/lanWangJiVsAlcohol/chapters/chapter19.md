The loud noise of the fierce fight quickly drew the other guests from their rooms upstairs. They had a clear view of the main hall downstairs, where the guests had stopped their revelry and were gazing at the source of the disturbance.

They all watched in bewilderment as, one by one, the bouncers, seemingly unable to break up the brawl, were thrown out of the room, landing in the hall amid screams.

One of the men fell near where Wèi WúXiàn was sitting, surrounded by the young ladies. The man’s face was covered in bruises; the corner of his mouth was swollen, and one or two bumps marred his forehead.

As he sat up, his gaze fell on Wèi WúXiàn. He was stunned for a moment, then suddenly pointed at him and began, “It’s this man who brought the one causing–”

A piercing shriek coming from upstairs interrupted him, immediately followed by a dishevelled man being hurled downstairs and landing heavily on top of him.

Standing upstairs, Jīn Líng snorted coldly, then leapt down into the main hall. Apart from getting his hair slightly messy and his clothes a little bit dusty, he was unscathed.

Wèi WúXiàn put down his cup of wine and lazily said, “You’re so slow.”

Jīn Líng glared at him. Not long ago, he had fought four men, one after another. He hadn’t even had time to catch his breath before he had to fight again.

The madam realised that the two gentlemen were there to cause trouble and quickly summoned more of her men to deal with them.

Despite being beaten to a pulp, Jīn Chǎn was still cursing under his breath.

Wèi WúXiàn roughly estimated the number of men coming to fight them, then stood up and walked over to Jīn Líng.

“We still have plenty of time,” he said. “Are you still up for a fight?”

Jīn Líng, whose true face was concealed by Wèi WúXiàn’s talisman, had no concerns about his identity being revealed and was delighted to keep fighting.

He replied, “You can just sit there and keep drinking.”

Lying on the floor, Jīn Chǎn struggled to turn his head to look at Jīn Líng, then shouted, “You...you have some nerve! Do you know who I am? Everyone working here is under my father’s command.”

Having had enough, Jīn Líng stamped on his head and said, “You’re a disgrace!”

Wèi WúXiàn patted him on the shoulder and said, “Since you still have the energy to carry on fighting, I’ll keep an eye on Jīn Chǎn for you.”

“An opportunity like this–when you can fight someone to your heart’s content–is hard to come by,” he added. “Enjoy yourself while I drink wine and count the number of jars I will have consumed by the time you knock them all out.”

Exuding confidence, Jīn Líng turned around and kicked two of his opponents who were charging at him, sending them flying backwards.

His eyes were filled with the same arrogance as Wèi WúXiàn’s. In a booming voice, he said, “In that case, you’d better hurry up and drink!”

So he did. However, after he had finished one jar of wine, Wèi WúXiàn lost interest in drinking, regardless of the fact that it was good wine.

He moved his chair in front of the main entrance and, before sitting down, used a talisman to seal it completely. He then continued watching Jīn Líng fighting several enforcers of the brothel until dawn.

The madam, seeing her men defeated, launched a flare so that people from the LánlíngJīn Sect would come to her aid.

Upon arriving and seeing Lán WàngJī standing outside the building with his imposing figure, those who had rushed over in response to the signal dared not approach.

Wèi WúXiàn blinked as he stepped outside. No wonder no one had tried to barge in – Lán WàngJī was guarding the entrance.

Jīn Líng also came out and was surprised to see that it was already bright outside and that he had to rush back to Jīnlíntái.

He wanted to take Jīn Chǎn with him, but Wèi WúXiàn stopped him, saying, “Let me keep him for a while. I have something to ask him. When I’m done, I’ll bring him to you.”

Jīn Líng was puzzled by the request; nevertheless, he agreed without asking any questions. He walked over to Lán WàngJī and took back his sword from him, thanking him before taking his leave.

After walking some distance, Jīn Líng remembered to tear off the talisman concealing his true face. Then he met the YúnmèngJiāng disciples who were looking for him, along with Xiānzǐ, who had been guiding them by following Jīn Líng’s scent.

Jīn Líng learned from them that his uncle had captured three of the men who had ambushed him the previous night and that the situation in Jīnlíntái had become even more chaotic as they spoke.

In just one night, so many things had happened. After telling Jiāng Chéng the whole story and feeling more at ease around him, Jīn Líng suddenly felt exhausted – the fatigue that had been building throughout the night finally caught up with him.

Jiāng Chéng had been listening quietly the whole time. Jīn Líng was not only worn out but also drowsy. Through his blurred vision, he could not make out Jiāng Chéng’s expression.

Since his uncle was deep in thought and apparently had no more questions to ask him, Jīn Líng stretched and prepared to go to his room for a nap.

“A-Líng,” Jiāng Chéng suddenly called out. “Where is Wèi WúXiàn as we speak?”

Yawning, Jīn Líng replied, “Since he said he’d bring Jīn Chǎn later, he should still be in Lánlíng City.”

Jiāng Chéng remained silent for a moment. After a brief hesitation, he said, “You ought to thank him this time.”

Jīn Líng was so dumbfounded that all his drowsiness vanished. He exclaimed, “Uncle, what did you just say?”

Wèi WúXiàn indeed deserved his thanks for saving him, but Jīn Líng had never expected his uncle to suggest it.

Feeling as though a weight had suddenly been lifted from his shoulders, Jīn Líng smiled and asked, “Uncle, do you no longer hate him?”

Jiāng Chéng stared at Jīn Líng with wide eyes before asking in a low voice, “A-Líng, don’t you hate him?”

Despite the truth having been brought to light, Wèi WúXiàn was still blamed for the deaths of Jīn Líng’s parents.

Jiāng Chéng knew how much Jīn Líng had longed for his parents and could not help but hate Wèi WúXiàn. He had also missed his parents, but he had buried all those feelings deep inside.

While Wèi WúXiàn could endure any hardship without a single complaint, Jiāng Chéng simply couldn’t.

The golden core residing in his dantian had been a great support to him time and time again, so Jiāng Chéng could not bring himself to continue harbouring hatred towards its former owner – the boy in his memories who was always one step ahead of him.

Scratching his head, Jīn Líng replied, “Actually, I’ve stopped holding a grudge against him a long time ago. To be honest, I can’t keep blaming him – he had already died once.”

He then added, “I’ve always thought that you still hated him, so I...”

Choosing his words carefully, Jīn Líng paused before continuing, “...so I just feel that if you find out I no longer hate him, wouldn’t it be harder for you to bear it on your own?”

Jiāng Chéng’s pupils dilated sharply; his lips parted, but no words came out. He slowly clenched his fists and closed his eyes for a brief moment.

When he reopened them and spoke, his voice sounded hoarse. “You have your own thoughts and opinions. They don’t need to be similar to mine.”

The lingering resentment that had been weighing him down was gradually fading away.

He then added, “You don’t have to hate him if you don’t feel like it.”

The autumn wind blew desolately as the sun set. Yet the afterglow was still exceptionally intense and brilliant, vividly colouring the passing clouds.

Through an interplay of movement and stillness, their encounter gave rise to a spectacular, colourful sunset.

Standing quietly at the top of the Jīnlíntái’s imposing outdoor staircase, Jīn Líng watched Jiāng Chéng and his disciples fly away upon their swords.

He then stretched, easing his sore muscles. Having fought all night and then been busy since morning, it was time for him to get some well-deserved rest.

As soon as he returned to his room, Jīn Líng took a bath, washing away the sweat and dust from the previous night.

It was not yet evening when he got out of the bath. His drowsiness having disappeared, Jīn Líng couldn’t help but reflect on his uncle’s words regarding Wèi WúXiàn.

A myriad of emotions welled up within him as he suddenly realised that, until now, he had been ungrateful to Wèi WúXiàn. Each time when they parted after spending time Night Hunting together, Jīn Líng never expressed his gratitude to him.

In addition, on many occasions, Wèi WúXiàn came to his rescue. He even let bygones be bygones.

Sitting at the table with his chin propped on one hand while lightly tapping his teacup, Jīn Líng recalled Wèi WúXiàn’s remark and had to admit that Wèi WúXiàn was right – apart from Night Hunting, there were really few opportunities for him to fight an opponent without holding back.

If Wèi WúXiàn happened to bring Jīn Chǎn in one piece, he would fight him once more to vent his anger.

Jīn Líng felt a twinge of regret and slumped over the table out of boredom. How could he have been so thoughtless? He should have at least asked Wèi WúXiàn why he wanted to keep Jīn Chǎn for a while.

At that moment, an underling came to report. He said, “Jīn Zōng-zhǔ, two GūsūLán disciples have just arrived. They brought Jīn Chǎn with them and asked to see you. Would you like to receive them now?”

By the time the underling had finished speaking, Jīn Líng had already rushed out of the room. As soon as he stepped outside, he saw Lán SīZhuī and Lán JǐngYí dragging the unruly Xiǎo-Píngguǒ. Jīn Chǎn was tied up and sitting on the donkey’s back.

Upon seeing Jīn Líng, Xiǎo-Píngguǒ suddenly became excited. It immediately reared up and threw Jīn Chǎn off its back. He landed on the ground with a thud.

Xiǎo-Píngguǒ then brayed as though to express its displeasure at having to carry Jīn Chǎn against its will.

Puzzled, Lán JǐngYí stuffed an apple into its mouth and said, “This donkey’s temper is getting worse. It’s not like it was carrying its enemy, so where does all this resentment come from?”

Jīn Líng was at a loss for words. He beckoned for his underlings to take Jīn Chǎn into custody, then invited his two companions to his room, where a servant brought them some tea and discreetly withdrew.

“When did the two of you arrive? Where is Wèi WúXiàn?” Jīn Líng asked impatiently.

Lán JǐngYí just rolled his eyes.

“We arrived in the morning,” Lán SīZhuī replied. “Zéwú-Jūn assigned us, along with other disciples, to escort people who had been treated at Yúnshēn Bùzhīchù back to their homes.”

“Why didn’t you come to see me then?” Jīn Líng asked.

Lán JǐngYí drank his cup of tea in one gulp before replying, “The relatives of the people we brought back had kept asking us questions and thanking us. We couldn’t take our leave until the afternoon.”

Lán SīZhuī added, “After that, we decided to come and see you, but on our way here, we ran into Wèi-qiánbèi and HánGuāng-Jūn. They said you might be busy and asked us to book rooms at an inn for all of us to stay in, then come and find you later.”

“Did Wèi WúXiàn say anything else when he told you to bring Jīn Chǎn over?” Jīn Líng asked.

Lán SīZhuī replied, “He said Jīn Chǎn had somehow obtained the research manuscripts he had written when he was at the Burial Mounds.”

After the discovery of the monster that had caused chaos among ordinary people, Wèi WúXiàn had started to suspect that Jīn Chǎn’s experiments on the monster stemmed from his manuscripts. That was why he had asked Jīn Líng to leave Jīn Chǎn with him so that he could interrogate him.

Lán SīZhuī added, “He also said that if you don’t want those manuscripts, it’s better to destroy them so that they won’t fall into someone else’s hands.”

Jīn Líng suddenly recalled that when Jīn GuāngYáo was still alive, he had amassed a collection of rare items, and Wèi WúXiàn’s manuscripts were among them.

Those manuscripts were beyond anyone’s comprehension, and hence they had been put into storage. Little did anyone know that Jīn Chǎn had set his sights on them and eventually managed to steal them.

Seeing Jīn Líng lost in thought, Lán JǐngYí asked suspiciously, “Hey, don’t tell me you’re thinking of keeping the manuscripts, are you?”

Jīn Líng glared at him, then replied, “Of course not. I was wondering whether to give them back to Wèi WúXiàn.”

“I asked Wèi-qiánbèi if he wanted to claim back his manuscripts, but he said he no longer needed them,” Lán SīZhuī said. “You can just destroy them.”

Jīn Líng let out a sigh of relief. He stood up and walked to the door, saying, “In that case, come with me. Let’s destroy them straight away so as to prevent a similar incident from happening again.”

“Why are you in such a hurry?” Lán JǐngYí said as he poured himself another cup of tea. “Wèi-qiánbèi said you had been kidnapped in the middle of the night. Afterwards, you spent all night fighting several villains at a brothel. Aren’t you tired?”

At the mention of the brothel, Jīn Líng almost stumbled over the threshold.

Lán SīZhuī felt awkward and thought, “Unwittingly, JǐngYí hit where it hurts.”
