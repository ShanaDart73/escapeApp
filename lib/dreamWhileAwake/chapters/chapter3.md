The strong smell of medicine jolted him back to his senses. He walked to the bed and glanced at the wounds on Wèi WúXiàn’s back—a sight that made his heart ache.

Suppressing the tears welling up in his eyes, Lán WàngJī asked, “Has your bandage been changed?”

“It’s been changed,” Wèi WúXiàn replied. “Your brother comes every day to change my bandage.”

Wèi WúXiàn’s voice was still hoarse, but he didn’t want to worry Lán WàngJī, so he spoke in a cheerful tone.

Lán WàngJī could only nod, as he had nothing else to say. Yet there was something he wanted to ask Wèi WúXiàn—what had happened to his Golden Core?

Guessing that it must be an unpleasant memory for Wèi WúXiàn, Lán WàngJī couldn’t bring himself to ask about it.

Besides, Wèi WúXiàn had never mentioned it before, so it was likely that he didn’t want others to know about it.

Since he had kept it buried deep in his heart, Lán WàngJī didn’t wish to open his wound either.

Unlike Lán WàngJī, Wèi WúXiàn usually never stopped talking. However, at that moment, he remained quiet, and a long silence fell between them.

In fact, Wèi WúXiàn had many questions he wanted to ask Lán WàngJī. He was certain that Lán WàngJī must know about his Golden Core. Had he told anyone else about it? Had the situation of the Wen clansmen been settled? How many people knew about the soul swapping? And what about the injuries on his back?

However, as he watched his own face—which usually wore a smile—now devoid of expression, Wèi WúXiàn found his courage failing him.

“No wonder people out there paint Yílíng Lǎozǔ as a fierce, menacing figure,” Wèi WúXiàn muttered to himself. “It turns out that I really do look quite scary when I’m not smiling. But then again, I’m hardly ever as aloof and morose as Lán Zhàn, am I?”

Wèi WúXiàn rolled his eyes, finding the prolonged silence quite awkward. He took a roundabout approach and asked, “Lán Zhàn, how did you get here?”

“On foot,” Lán WàngJī replied.

After a short pause, he added, “I brought the Wen clansmen along with me. Right now, Xiōng-zhǎng is making arrangements for them.”

Wèi WúXiàn’s jaw dropped in surprise—he hadn’t expected Lán WàngJī to go so far as to bring all the Wen clansmen to Yúnshēn Bùzhīchù.

He then said, “Thank you.”

Lán WàngJī shook his head. “There’s no need to thank me. It was my responsibility to take care of them.”

Wèi WúXiàn was stunned, but then he smiled and said, “HánGuāng-Jūn, you’ve never disappointed me.”

Upon hearing this, Lán WàngJī’s eyes lit up slightly. Then, the very next instant, the light in his eyes seemed to vanish as though it had never been there.

Lán WàngJī remained silent, and Wèi WúXiàn didn’t know how to continue the conversation. He scratched his head and ventured, “Did you run into trouble on your way here?”

“I did.”

“Really?!” Wèi WúXiàn’s heart tightened.

“The money ran out,” Lán WàngJī replied.

“Ah?” Wèi WúXiàn blinked.

Lán WàngJī thought Wèi WúXiàn hadn’t heard him clearly, so he repeated, “The money for travel expenses ran out.”

Wèi WúXiàn was speechless. “Should I be blamed for being poor?” he thought.

He remained silent for a while, picturing the situation—HánGuāng-Jūn, who seemed above the cares of mundane affairs, actually had moments when he found himself troubled by the lack of money!

Wèi WúXiàn suddenly felt the urge to laugh out loud.

“Wait a minute,” he thought. “What was I about to ask him again?”

He cleared his throat and asked, “Did you encounter any other trouble?”

“Like what?” Lán WàngJī asked.

“For example, being recognised by some cultivators,” Wèi WúXiàn replied. “The face of Yílíng Lǎozǔ has made quite an impression on the cultivation world lately.”

“Yes, it happened,” Lán WàngJī replied.

Seeing that Wèi WúXiàn had suddenly become nervous, Lán WàngJī reassured him, saying, “But it didn’t cause much trouble.”

“Oh,” Wèi WúXiàn murmured. Then he wondered, “Was he able to deal with any trouble along the way without Bìchén or WàngJī? Has Lán Zhàn really become this powerful?”

Unable to contain his curiosity, Wèi WúXiàn asked, “So, how did you deal with those people?”

“I used the Silent Spell on them,” Lán WàngJī replied.

He then glanced at Wèi WúXiàn’s lips but immediately felt awkward. He still hadn’t got used to seeing his own face whenever he looked at Wèi WúXiàn.

He seemed to notice his lips twitching slightly, as though Wèi WúXiàn was trying hard to keep them pressed together.

Wèi WúXiàn was, in fact, holding back laughter. But the next moment, he suddenly burst out laughing, pounding his fists against his pillow.

He had forgotten about the gruesome injuries on his back, and his sudden movements tugged at them, causing him to hiss in pain.

“Don’t move around so much.” Lán WàngJī hurriedly held Wèi WúXiàn’s hands down.

“I’m fine,” Wèi WúXiàn replied. “Lán Zhàn, you crack me up.”

Wèi WúXiàn couldn’t stop laughing, despite the excruciating pain in his back.

Lán WàngJī watched him gasp for breath between bouts of laughter, worried that he would breathe his last. He didn’t know what to do to stop Wèi WúXiàn from laughing so hard, so he could only remind him to stay still.

“Try not to move too much,” Lán WàngJī warned him.

After forcing himself to calm down, Wèi WúXiàn replied, “All right. I’ll remain still.”

As soon as his laughter had subsided, a thought crossed his mind, which made him lose control once more.

He began, “In other words, the instant someone recognised you, or should I say, recognised Yílíng Lǎozǔ, you silenced them using the Silent Spell!”

Wèi WúXiàn burst out laughing before continuing, “In a few days, I’m afraid that people out there will be pinning yet another label on me, saying, ‘That Yílíng Lǎozǔ is so evil, using some kind of demonic spell on whoever sees him and leaving them unable to speak ever again!’”

“Silent Spell has a time limit,” Lán WàngJī reminded him.

Laughing, Wèi WúXiàn replied, “I was just joking. Why are you taking it so seriously?”

The gloom in his heart seemed to have lifted considerably after a good laugh.

Glancing at Lán WàngJī and seeing his own face turn expressionless, Wèi WúXiàn suddenly stretched out his arm and placed two fingers at either corner of Lán WàngJī’s mouth, then lifted them into a smile.

Caught off guard, Lán WàngJī’s eyes widened in surprise.

“That’s more like it,” Wèi WúXiàn remarked. “This face should be smiling. Otherwise, how could it possibly reflect even a small fraction of my handsomeness?”

At that precise moment, Lán XīChén pushed open the door and stepped inside. What he saw was “Lán WàngJī” lying face down on the bed, his fingers poking “Wèi WúXiàn” in the face while his own face beamed with a radiant smile.

Feeling out of place, Lán XīChén averted his gaze, then raised his fist to his lips and gave a soft cough.

At the sound of the cough, Lán WàngJī sprang to his feet as though he had been caught doing something shameful. A faint blush spread across his cheeks as he bowed and greeted him, “Xiōng-zhǎng.”

Wèi WúXiàn also adopted a more serious expression and said, “Zéwú-Jūn.”

“I seem to have disturbed you,” Lán XīChén said.

Smiling, he went on, “I’ve already provided accommodation for the Wen clansmen. They are currently in the guest house. I had someone prepare some food and sent it to them.”

“WàngJī,” he continued, “why don’t you get yourself ready so that you can come with me to see Uncle?”

Although Lán Qǐrén had never been fond of anything related to Wèi WúXiàn, now that the soul-swapping incident had occurred, he ought to be informed.

Lán WàngJī replied, “All right.”

Wèi WúXiàn thought to himself that Lán Qǐrén was knowledgeable and well-informed and might have some insight into the bizarre situation.

He then said, “I’ll go with you.”

However, Lán XīChén and Lán WàngJī both shook their heads simultaneously.

Lán WàngJī said, “You have severe injuries on your back, so it’s not wise to move about. Besides...” He paused before continuing, “I am, I mean, since you currently inhabit my body, you are under house arrest and are not allowed to step outside the room.”

“House arrest?!” Wèi WúXiàn thought in bewilderment. “Has Lán Zhàn been punished? Surely not! He always conducts himself with such propriety and dignity. What kind of mistake he could possibly have made?”

“Wait a moment,” he wondered. “These injuries on his back... Could they be the result of his being beaten as part of his punishment?”

“Good heavens! What kind of disciplinary instrument could have caused such excruciating pain? Back when Lady Yu lashed me with Zǐdiàn*, it didn’t hurt this much.”

<small>Zǐdiàn means purple electric whip.</small>

Wèi WúXiàn was lost in thought, and, by the time he snapped out of it, Lán XīChén had already left Jingshi, while Lán WàngJī was busy retrieving a set of clean clothes from the wardrobe.

He licked his dry lips and began, “Um... Lán Zhàn.”

“What is it?” Lán WàngJī asked.

“How did you get these injuries on your back?” Wèi WúXiàn asked.

After a brief hesitation, Lán WàngJī replied, “Having committed a transgression, I must accept the punishment.”

“So, it’s indeed the case,” Wèi WúXiàn thought. “But... what kind of mistake could possibly warrant such a brutal beating as punishment? I simply can’t fathom it.”

Judging by Lán WàngJī’s expression, he didn’t seem keen on elaborating further on the matter, so Wèi WúXiàn could only utter a soft “Oh.”

Noticing that Wèi WúXiàn had no further questions, Lán WàngJī took the clean clothes, picked up the hot water brought by a servant and went behind the folding screen to prepare for a bath.

He untied the red ribbon and held it in his hands, gazing at it for a while before hanging it on the rack. He then began to remove his clothes, one item at a time, taking great care not to let his fingers come into contact with his skin.

Having stripped off all his clothes, Lán WàngJī stepped into the bath and sank into the water. Then his mind began to race once more.

“This body is not mine,” he thought. “It belongs to Wèi Yīng.”

Indeed, he was occupying the body of the person he had always longed for but had never dared to touch.

After sitting there motionless for quite a while, Lán WàngJī finally made up his mind. He picked up a wooden ladle from the bucket, scooped up some water and slowly poured it over his head. He did the same with his shoulders and arms, pouring water over his body.

He then took a cloth and began to scrub himself, starting from his face and then gradually working his way down.

As he reached his chest, his fingertips sensed an uneven surface through the cloth. It was the burn scar in the shape of a sun-like pattern.

Lán WàngJī felt a pang in his heart and softened his movements as he rubbed the scar.

Moving further down, Lán WàngJī discovered two scars on the abdomen. Judging by their appearance, both seemed to have been inflicted by a blade.

One scar was on the left side of the abdomen and looked relatively fresh. Wèi WúXiàn had probably sustained it during his fight with Jiāng Chéng, when Wèi WúXiàn defected from the YúnmèngJiāng Sect.

The other scar had already blended in with the surrounding skin. It was located below the belly button, exactly where the dantian was, and where his Golden Core was supposed to reside.

Lán WàngJī was stunned because the scar indicated that Wèi WúXiàn’s Golden Core had been cut out by someone rather than dissolved by Wen Zhuliu, as he had first thought.

When he finished bathing, he dressed and then stepped out from behind the folding screen. He saw that Wèi WúXiàn was still awake, his eyes darting about the room in sheer boredom.

“Why aren’t you sleeping?” Lán WàngJī asked.

“I’ve been sleeping for seven or eight hours a day for the past few days,” Wèi WúXiàn replied. “I’ve already had plenty of sleep. How can I fall asleep again so soon after waking up?”

In fact, with such severe injuries, falling asleep wasn’t simple. He hadn’t actually been sleeping for hours, as he claimed, but had instead been in a semi-conscious state due to his fever.

Lán WàngJī was aware of that. He also knew that Wèi WúXiàn didn’t want him to feel guilty, so he said nothing.

After placing Wèi WúXiàn’s clothes in the bamboo basket, Lán WàngJī walked over to the bed and laid out Wèi WúXiàn’s belongings—namely, Chénqíng, Suíbiàn and Yīn-Hǔfú*.
